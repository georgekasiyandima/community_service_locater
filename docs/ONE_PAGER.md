# Ubuntu Code — One-pager (plain language)

*Use this as the source for a redesigned PDF. It is written so a priest, a head teacher, a councillor or a shop owner can read it in three minutes. No jargon required.*

---

## Who we are

Ubuntu Code builds digital tools for the people and organisations of Manicaland.

It was started by George Kasiyandima, a full-stack developer from Manicaland, together with fellow developers. We work with churches, schools, civic groups, local councils, and small businesses in rural areas and small towns.

---

## Why this matters

Too many organisations and shops that do important work still run on scattered paper, WhatsApp rumours, and no website. That makes it hard to be found, hard to coordinate, and hard to serve the next person who walks in.

We build software that fits real conditions: uneven connectivity, tight budgets, and phones first — not offices full of computers.

---

## Proof we already shipped

We designed and launched the live website for **Caritas Mutare** ([www.caritasmutare.org](https://www.caritasmutare.org)) — the Diocese of Mutare’s social action arm — from the first conversation through to launch.

A second, living proof is the **Community Service Locator** for Bvumba Ward 14 and Sherukuru Ward 15: [ubuntu-code-locator.onrender.com](https://ubuntu-code-locator.onrender.com). It tells a ward which clinic is open, which borehole is working, and what is happening at the school.

## What we can do for you

We begin with a **free conversation**. We will not sell you a system before we understand your actual problem.

### For churches, schools, NGOs and councils

- A clear website or information page people can actually load
- Directories and notice tools (who is open, who to call, what changed)
- Civic information that lives on WhatsApp, not only on a poster

### For small businesses and groups

- A simple website or WhatsApp catalogue
- Booking and basic shop records
- Farm notes, market links, and records for savings groups or cooperatives
- A small custom tool when the ready-made apps do not fit

If a word like “SaaS” appears in an older draft, it only means: a tool your team can keep using, not a file that dies on one laptop.
