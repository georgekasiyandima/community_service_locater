The problem

The information exists. The channel does not.

Someone in Chikumbu should not walk to Sherukuru Clinic only to find the nurse is at district training. The Mukoyi borehole can break on a Tuesday and Hakuziwi still hears it is working on Thursday. A school at Zambe or Sherukuru can close for a break and the rumour travels faster than the notice.

This is not a lack of knowledge. It is a lack of one trusted, current place to ask — on a feature of life that already works in rural Zimbabwe: WhatsApp.

What this prototype proves

A community member can text one word and get a current answer in seconds.

A councillor, parish rep or teacher can change a status from their own phone, without a workshop.

Ubuntu Code can ship a tool that fits local budgets, connectivity, and trust — then grow it ward by ward.

How it works

No app to install. No account to create.

1

Ask

Reply 14 for Bvumba or 15 for Sherukuru, then CLINIC, BOREHOLE, SCHOOL, CHURCH, POLICE or SHOP.

2

Trust a neighbour

One or two known numbers — a ward councillor, a parish representative, a teacher — are allowed to update status.

3

Correct it in a minute

They send UPDATE BOREHOLE2 WORKING. The next person who asks about Mukoyi sees the change.
